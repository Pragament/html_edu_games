Understand lines, spaces & symbols
Five lines
From bottom to top: E, G, B, D, F. Notes on higher lines sound higher.
Four spaces
From bottom to top: F, A, C, E. Notes also fit in the spaces between lines.
G, A & B
G is on the second line from the bottom, A is in the second space, and B is on the middle line. On the keyboard, G and A sit between the three black keys, and B sits immediately right of the group.
Ledger line
A short extra line extends the staff. Middle C is on the first ledger line below it; D is just above that line, and E is on the bottom staff line.
𝄞 Treble clef
The curl marks the G line (second line from the bottom), helping us name every note.
Note head, stem & flag
The oval head’s position gives the note name. In our beat convention: a filled head with a stem is a quarter note (1 beat); add a flag for an eighth note (½ beat). A hollow head with a stem is a half note (2 beats); without a stem it is a whole note (4 beats).
♯ Sharp
Raises a note by one semitone. C♯ and D♯ are the two black keys here.
Pitch & instrument
This staff uses middle C as its reading reference. Pitch sets move the sound by octaves; some instruments shift register. Tabla uses drum hits, so the staff acts as a key-and-rhythm guide rather than drum pitch notation.
