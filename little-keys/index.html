Understand lines, spaces & symbols
Five lines
From bottom to top: E, G, B, D, F. Notes on higher lines sound higher.
Four spaces
From bottom to top: F, A, C, E. Notes also fit in the spaces between lines.
G, A & B
G is on the second line from the bottom, A is in the second space, and B is on the middle line. On the keyboard, G and A sit between the three black keys, and B sits immediately right of the group.
Ledger line
A short extra line extends the staff. Middle C is on the first ledger line below it; D is just above that line, and E is on the bottom staff line.
𝄞 Treble clef
The curl marks the G line (second line from the bottom), helping us name every note.
Note head, stem & flag
The oval head’s position gives the note name. In our beat convention: a filled head with a stem is a quarter note (1 beat); add a flag for an eighth note (½ beat). A hollow head with a stem is a half note (2 beats); without a stem it is a whole note (4 beats).
♯ Sharp
Raises a note by one semitone. C♯ and D♯ are the two black keys here.
Pitch & instrument
This staff uses middle C as its reading reference. Pitch sets move the sound by octaves; some instruments shift register. Tabla uses drum hits, so the staff acts as a key-and-rhythm guide rather than drum pitch notation.
TRY A WIND INSTRUMENT

Play on recorder

A recorder is an end-blown flute. This starter guide uses soprano recorder fingerings; concert flutes and bansuris use different fingerings.

● Covered hole○ Open hole

  1. Left hand at the top; right hand below. Hole 0 is on the back.
  2. Cover holes fully with soft finger pads. Keep your right hand supporting the recorder without closing holes 4–7 for B, A or G.
  3. Blow gently and steadily. Begin each note with a soft “tu”; avoid a hard blast of air.
  4. For B → A, add your middle finger. For A → G, add your ring finger. Lift them to go back up.

The diagram follows listening, your played notes, and selected staff notes. Real recorder sound is not detected by the app. Soprano recorder music sounds an octave above its written notation; the starter button selects the high pitch set and the synthesized Flute tone for a closer sound.

BREATHE A MELODY

Play on harmonica

Use a 10-hole diatonic harmonica in C, standard Richter tuning. Keep the numbers upright with hole 1 on your left. Chromatic, tremolo, and differently tuned harmonicas need their own charts.

+ = blow OUT · − = draw IN. “5+” also means blow hole 5. Some tabs write a plain “4” for draw; this app uses “−4” to make the direction clear.

Song tabs with lyrics
  1. Relax your lips and make a small opening to isolate one hole.
  2. Use gentle, steady breath. Draw means breathing in through the instrument, without a hard suck.
  3. For E → D → C: blow 5, draw 4, blow 4. Keep each note for its written beat length.
  4. On repeated notes, use a light “ta” articulation to separate the notes.

The tabs follow listening, app keys, and selected staff notes. Real harmonica sound is not detected. Tabs use the middle register (C5 and above), with upper-register tabs for octave variations where available. The starter selects the high pitch set to match; changing the app’s pitch does not retune a real C harmonica. Two-hand piano accompaniment controls are separate from harmonica technique.

STRIKE, BOUNCE & KEEP THE BEAT

Play on percussion

Mallets play the melody; a snare plays the rhythm. Use note-labelled bars on a glockenspiel, xylophone or similar pitched percussion instrument. A snare stroke has no C/D/E note name. Use these simple beginner patterns to practise a melody part and a separate snare part.

Click a bar to preview a ringing mallet-style sound. Follow the staff’s note lengths on a real instrument: strike once and let it ring, rather than holding a mallet against the bar. The app’s keyboard hold meter measures key presses. The starter uses the synthesized Santoor tone as a mallet-style sound; it is not a sampled glockenspiel.

Snare drum · right & left hands

4/4: four quarter-note beats per bar. R = right stroke, L = left stroke, the rest symbol = quarter rest (one silent beat). Play one stroke per marked beat. Use a relaxed grip and let each stick rebound.

Tap a pad or press J / K

Choose a pattern. A four-beat count-in comes before each exercise. The Tempo setting controls the beat.

The snare exercise has its own two-bar score. It does not advance the melody. Timing uses pad clicks or J/K presses; real sticks and microphone sound are not detected. Try the melody and snare as separate parts first, then play together with a friend.
